# How to talk about Understory Collaborative

An internal reference so anyone on the team can describe what UC does, in one voice. A draft
to react to. Where a real answer is not settled yet, it says **[webs decides]** rather than
guessing.

**Two voices to keep straight.** This guide is written in the house style we use with each
other: scannable, tabular, plain. The **quoted lines** in it are outward-facing, so they
follow the website voice gate (`design-system/VOICE.md`): plain, unhurried, no self-certifying
words, no slop, no naming competitors.

---

## The one-liner

> We are technical product managers. We help you decide what to build and in what order, we
> help your team build it, and we step back once they can run it without us.

If you say one thing, say that.

---

## What makes us different (the wedge)

Most outside help is one half of the job. We are the rarer hire that does both, and then
leaves.

| The two half-hires | What we do instead |
|---|---|
| A shop that builds exactly what you spec and never asks whether it is the right thing | We decide what is worth building, then build it |
| An advisor with a deck full of opinions who has never opened the codebase | We stay close enough to the code to review the pull request |
| Help that keeps you dependent | The proof we did the job is that you no longer need us |

The belief underneath it: **you do not have to choose between a weak generalist and a narrow
specialist.** A technical product manager is a jack of all trades who carries real depth, from
market research, enablement, and go-to-market to reading the technical documentation and
sitting in a team's challenge reviews. There are not many people who do this well.

---

## Who we help

The person who calls us has one of three situations, and usually a title like CTO, VP of
Engineering, or head of a product org.

| Situation | What it sounds like |
|---|---|
| A project is behind | "The date keeps slipping, and no one can say what actually ships." |
| A team is struggling | "My team keeps missing, and I need to know whether it can be saved." |
| AI has gotten away from them | "AI is loose in my org, and it is more risk and noise than help." |

We stay with that buyer. We do not chase portfolio-scale transformation work as the front
door, even though we can take it on once we are in.

## What we offer: Design, Build, Ship

Same three buckets the homepage names. Each is a slice of what a technical product manager
owns.

| Offer | The job | Talk-track |
|---|---|---|
| **Design** | What to build | "We scope the real first version and get it shipped, then hand the project back so your team can steer it." |
| **Build** | How to build it | "We evaluate a struggling team, clear the small irritants first, and either get it back to a steady pace or give you a defensible answer that it cannot." |
| **Ship** | How to ship it | "We put guardrails around AI for security, PII, and spend, get adoption to the people still refusing, and kill the slop." |

**Pricing:** the only customer-facing price is the $50 office hours. Everything else is scoped
after a short conversation. Do **not** quote a project price off a form or a hallway chat.

| Rung | What it is | Free or paid | It leads to |
|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | Q&A, one real question answered | Free | Office hours or a conversation |

| 4 | Design, Build, or Ship engagement | Paid, scoped | — |

## Elevator pitches at three lengths

**Ten seconds:**
> We are technical product managers. We help you figure out what to build, help your team
> build it, and leave once they can run it without us.

**Thirty seconds:**
> Most outside help is one half of the job. A shop that builds what you spec without asking if
> it is right, or an advisor with opinions who has never opened your codebase. We do both. When
> a project is behind or a team is stuck, we come in, get it moving, and step back once your
> team can carry it.

**Two minutes:** open with the thirty-second version, then add the three situations we take
on (a project behind, a team struggling, AI that has gotten away from them), how someone
starts (the free assessment, then office hours, then a scoped engagement), and one real
example from the offer pages (a delivery rescue, a team turnaround, or the AI adoption
method). Close on the point that we leave the team stronger than we found it.

## Words we use, words we avoid

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Decide what to build and in what order | Digital transformation, digital strategy |
| Work alongside your team | Synergy, seamless, end-to-end, unlock, leverage, elevate |
| Leave you able to run it without us | "AI swarm," "autonomous product velocity," any hype coinage |
| The messy middle; a jack of all trades with depth | Naming a competitor, ever (we may even subcontract with them) |
| Plain, specific, calm | "Honestly," "frankly," "the truth is" (they imply the rest is not) |

Educate, do not pitch. The client is the hero, not us.

## Handling common objections

Drawn from the personas. Answer plainly; do not oversell.

| They ask | We say |
|---|---|
| "How is this different from the last consultancy?" | "We do both halves of the job, and we leave. We will not sell you a rebuild you would then pay to run and maintain." |
| "Are you big enough for our scale?" | "We scope to a defined slice, embed with your team, and hand capability back. You work directly with senior people, not a stack of billable hours under a senior name." |
| "Do you actually know our industry?" | "Decades across healthcare, transportation, legal services, and education." |
| "Will you keep us dependent?" | "The proof we did the job is that you no longer need us." |
| "Will I get seniors or juniors?" | "Today you work directly with senior people. We do not run a pyramid where junior staff do the work under a senior name." The point is the model, and paying senior rates for junior output, never that junior people are lesser. Keep it present tense; do not promise senior-only forever. |

---

## What we never promise

- A price, a scope, or an outcome from a form submission. A form starts a conversation.
- A named client or case study. We tell the story with the numbers, no company named.
- A metric we have not checked. "A few questions" beats a "two minutes" we cannot defend.
- A rebuild the client does not need.
