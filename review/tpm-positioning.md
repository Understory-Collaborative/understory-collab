## The wedge, in one line

The rare generalist who is strategic enough to sequence the whole product and technical
enough to read the pull request, and who leaves your team stronger instead of dependent.

That is a technical product manager. Most firms sit on one side of it: the code shop that
optimizes craft and misses the whole product, or the strategy shop that ships a deck and
cannot execute. UC is the discipline that sits across both.

## Blindspot to claim (internal fuel, never quoted on-site)

The sharpest thing in the outside analysis was reading rivals by what they neglect: process,
people, and sustainability, not tech. Each neglect is a claim UC can make without naming a
single firm (the voice gate forbids naming competitors).

| The gap in the market | UC's positive claim (on-brand, no names) |
|---|---|
| Code-craft shops optimize engineering and miss the commercial whole; product owners just manage backlogs | A TPM owns the whole product, market and technical, not only the code |
| Billable-hours firms maximize utilization and burn the team out | We work toward the point where you no longer need us, and we watch whether the team can carry the pace |
| Both lean on hero individuals and junior pyramids, so knowledge concentrates in a few people | We find and unwind the load-bearing-person risk (this is the bus-factor axis the assessment is adding) |

This table is a strategy asset. It informs copy; it does not become copy.

## Why TPM fits the brand (not a pivot, a sharper name)

The TPM traits webs named are a near-restatement of UC's existing soul:

| TPM trait (webs) | UC value it already holds |
|---|---|
| Empowering, not jumping in to save | Guide, not hero. The proof we did the job is that they no longer need us. |
| Influencing without authority | The outsider who moves the org with no title to lean on |
| Skilled generalist, market and technical | "We understand both business drivers and technical constraints" |
| Soothsayer | The free assessment. We can see what is coming that you cannot yet. |
| Renaissance range; rejects weak-generalist-vs-specialist | The answer to the "mile wide, inch deep" objection in the personas |

The disagreement webs stated ("you can be a strong generalist, not only a weak one or a
specialist") is the whole positioning. The site should make that claim true with evidence,
not assert it.

## The TPM remit, mapped onto Design / Build / Ship

Path A keeps the storefront and recasts each door as part of what a TPM owns end to end. The
buyer still self-selects on the problem; the identity underneath is TPM.

| Door | Problem the buyer recognizes (storefront) | The TPM job underneath (identity) |
|---|---|---|
| **Design** — what to build | Scope keeps shifting; no one can name the first version | Deciding what to build and in what sequence; the soothsayer call on what matters now |
| **Build** — how to build it | The team keeps missing; can it be turned around | Moving a team without authority; removing the load-bearing-person risk; enablement over rescue |
| **Ship** — how to ship it | We could ship far more often; too much is by hand | Release cadence, lifecycle, and the sane-AI practices that raise a team's leverage |

## Where the responsibility list goes

webs's full TPM responsibility list (portfolio strategy, integration sequencing, multiyear
consolidation, go-to-market, internal and sales enablement, market strategy, industry and
team development, vendor and partner ecosystems, lifecycle management and deprecation) is
broad and portfolio-scale. Per P2 we are not moving the buyer upmarket to chase it. So it
serves two jobs, neither of which is a storefront menu:

1. **Proof of range.** On About and the offer pages, a few of these (integration sequencing,
   consolidation, lifecycle and deprecation, vendor ecosystems) are the vocabulary that
   *shows* the generalist is real, instead of claiming it.
2. **Intake-qualified scope.** The bigger portfolio-scale work stays available and is scoped
   at intake under "work with me." It is never the front door, so we do not reposition the
   business around a sale we cannot reliably win cold off a website.

**Home positioning band (names the wedge, no competitor):**
> Most technical work stalls in the gap between the people who can build it and the people who
> can decide what it is worth. We work in that gap. We are close enough to the code to review
> the pull request, and far enough back to see which fire actually matters this quarter.

**About, opening the identity:**
> The work we do has a name: technical product management. It is the practice of moving a
> product forward across the messy middle, deciding what to build and in what order,
> influencing teams we do not manage, and handing the whole thing back running so you can
> steer it without us.

**Range, shown not claimed (About or offer page):**
> On a given engagement that can mean sequencing which systems to integrate first, planning a
> consolidation that runs over years, or deciding what to sunset so the rest can move. The
> common thread is judgment about the whole product, not one slice of it.

## Resolved with webs (2026-08-19)

1. **The word:** use "technical product management" in full, in the identity layer. It owns
   the category.
2. **How-we-work section:** keep Root / Rise / Flourish; add a positioning band above it that
   names the discipline and states the wedge. (Reworking the method into Root/Rise/Flourish
   is a later option, not this pass.)
3. **Range vocabulary:** feature the triad that proves the generalist spans all three axes,
   each landing on the locked buyer's pain, rather than the most exotic-sounding duties:
   - **Technical** — what to integrate, why, and in what sequence (a behind project is
     usually a sequencing failure).
   - **Market / strategic** — product strategy, what's worth building now (the half the code
     shops miss).
   - **People / org** — internal enablement and team development (the empowerment soul; the
     anti-dependency proof).
   Deprecation / sunsetting and multiyear consolidation move to intake-qualified scope, not
   the shop window (nobody shops for a sunset; consolidation is portfolio-scale, past our
   buyer).
