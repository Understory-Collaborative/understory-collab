## What round 1 shows

1. **Buyers describe the problem and never the discipline.** They write "late," "behind,"
   "missing deadlines," "slow," "scope creep," "estimates," and "tech debt." The only
   people who say "technical product manager" are asking what the job is. The tagline and
   the one-line description (workstream 2) need the problem words; "technical product
   management" can stay in the identity layer, as `review/tpm-positioning.md` already says.
2. **Most askers are the person stuck in the middle, not the executive.** Engineers, PMs,
   and managers ask how to tell the boss, how to get leaders to care about tech debt, and
   how to handle a client who keeps adding scope. That matches the champion who carries
   Understory in (`review/tpm-positioning.md`, decision P2), and the posts should speak to
   them as well as to the CTO.
3. **Non-technical founders show up more than the positioning expects.** Three questions
   come from founders who can't tell whether their developers are slow. webs to decide
   whether that's an audience to write for.
4. **Three clusters have no answer on the site:** estimates, telling the boss it's late, and
   tech debt that leadership ignores. Each is a strong first post, and estimates connects to
   the confidence score webs already built.
5. **The ranking competition is vendor blogs.** A general search for "why does my
   engineering team keep missing deadlines" returned articles from tool vendors and
   agencies (Ciklum, Plaky, DeployFlow, a DEV Community post), not practitioners writing
   from experience. A post from someone who has run these teams has room to stand out.
